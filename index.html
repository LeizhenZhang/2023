About Me

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at the University of Louisiana at Lafayette . My research focuses on software engineering, software security, trustworthy artificial intelligence, and the evaluation of large language models.

My current research includes vulnerability detection, reliable and trustworthy AI for software engineering, large language model reasoning and evaluation, and machine learning for software and security applications.

Prior to joining the University of Louisiana at Lafayette, I received my M.S. in Computer Science from Old Dominion University . I received my bachelor's degree from China Agricultural University .

Research Experience

University of Louisiana at Lafayette

Graduate Teaching and Research Assistant
August 2024 – Present

  • LLM reasoning evaluation: Designed benchmarks and matched SAT/UNSAT evaluations to study reasoning reliability and shortcut learning. First author of “Satisfiability Solving with LLMs: A Matched-Pair Evaluation of Reasoning Capability” (FSE 2026).
  • Software vulnerability detection: Studied model evaluation, semantic program analysis, and robustness. First author of “Understanding Vulnerability Detection Difficulties, a New Metric Suite and an Explanation” (USENIX Security 2025 Poster Session).
  • VulValidate: Developed an LLM-agent-assisted framework for auditing function-level vulnerability labels with executable evidence. Audited 35,849 originally vulnerable-labeled instances across BigVul, PrimeVul, and DiverseVul, confirming 20,510 vulnerabilities and identifying 6,819 label corrections. Manuscript under review.
  • Model memorization: Co-author of “BLIA: Detect model memorization in binary classification model through passive Label Inference attack.” Manuscript under review.
